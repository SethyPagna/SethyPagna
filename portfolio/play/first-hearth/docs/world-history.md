# Meridian: a world with 997 years of disputed records

This is original fictional content expanded from master plan v0.5 §§20–22. Dates retained from that plan are marked **baseline**. Additional details are **proposals**. No event here is claimed to be executable merely because it has a date or location. The prototype uses one region around Stonebrook; the capital and wider provinces are future content.

## A compact playable geography

The **Stonebrook Reach** contains a farming village, a River-Reed seasonal settlement, Greyford's toll crossing, a Dawn Academy field archive, the old Thornwater evacuation road, and a ruined charitable hospice. These sites can share one regional map. Travel distances, encounter levels and exact grid coordinates belong in editable content data; the prose does not silently establish numerical simulation values.

Region quality means infrastructure, training access, threat pressure, faction reach and discovery requirements. It does not mean that rural people are morally or intellectually inferior. A capital contains weak recruits and opportunists as well as rare specialists. A remote community can contain a trained visitor, an exceptional local or a hidden history. Avoid universal star ranges that eliminate surprise.

## The River-Reed people

River-Reed is a proposed cultural self-name, not a monster faction or a fourth initial diplomacy faction. Several households move between fixed winter homes and summer reed-bank settlements. Assemblies select seasonal speakers; boat routes and flood records are shared responsibilities. There is disagreement between households over joining Stonebrook agreements, accepting Greyford fees and preserving fisheries. Their members speak ordinary dialogue, hold possessions, refuse orders and form relationships.

Most initial procedurally assembled River-Reed recruits have 1.0–2.0 potential, as requested for tribal populations. That distribution reflects this starting roster's training and recruitment pool, not a biological limit or cultural ranking. Owen's 2.5 potential is one explicit exception. Village and city background populations also include low-star workers. Low-star characters gain profession knowledge, equipment, experience and bonds; recruitment is not a program for replacing them when a capital opens.

Use role records rather than extra fixed hero dossiers for boatwrights, reed farmers, fishers, weather keepers and assembly delegates. A generated member can receive an English given name and surname from a configurable pool when created. Persist their identity thereafter. These records do not add an eleventh authored main hero. Monsters, wildlife, nonrecruitable people and recruitable people are distinct entity categories; “NPC” never means only monster.

The empire is approximately a millennium old. The current date is Year 997, not an inconsistent “exactly one thousand years since founding.” Later calendars may disagree about Year 0; retain one canonical simulation calendar and render local names as localization.

## Three regional factions, several points of view

**Stonebrook Council** wants food, security and independence. Farmers need debt relief; workshop owners fear losing imported tools; displaced people need representation. Supporting the council is not automatically supporting every household.

**Dawn Academy** wants knowledge recovery and institutional credibility. Reformers want disclosure; custodians fear dangerous replication; career officials fear responsibility. Access to its archive never grants blanket consent to experiment on people or domains.

**Greyford Compact** wants trade, order and toll control. Couriers want reliable routes, officials want stable revenue, and merchants disagree about exclusive contracts. An honest official and a corrupt official can both work here.

Charities, survivor associations and River-Reed assemblies are initial social groups attached to local content, not automatically additional full diplomacy systems. Add a faction only when it needs independent persistent relations and actions.

## Disaster design and historical memory

Beast tides follow migration pressure, damaged routes and environmental disturbance. Storms affect known exposed sites with warnings. Their detailed scheduler belongs in mechanics data; history explains why people interpret them differently. A tide can reopen a neglected evacuation road, expose records in a cellar or require cooperation between political rivals.

Aid earns favor from people who learn about it, after a real cost and outcome. Repeatedly manufacturing danger never creates unlimited favor. Protecting an exposed ferry can improve River-Reed trust without obliging its members to enlist. Origin Core protection remains dependable: disasters may threaten external assets and personnel, not secretly delete safe possessions.

Use damaged walls, repaired markers, competing commemorative plaques, ledger copies and ordinary family objects as history delivery. The player should learn enough to make a decision before reading an encyclopedia. A late archive discovery can reveal a motive or missing fact; it must not silently overwrite an earlier observed fact.

## Continuity guardrails

- Thornwater occurred 24 years before the present. Arin was nine, Mira ten, Elias twenty and Kael twenty-seven.
- Medical orders descend from Year 371; modern characters and their grandparents are not centuries old.
- Restoration institutions descend from 842; Seren joined a current successor in 984.
- The old archive split and its modern interpretation are different events. Evelyn's tutors did not personally edit records in 492.
- Kael's retreat, his false report and denied compensation are distinct judgments.
- Arin's brother's final fate is unresolved. Do not improvise a definitive reveal across unrelated quests.
- Regional history offers many causes. Avoid a single hidden cult explaining every famine, official decision and beast migration.
